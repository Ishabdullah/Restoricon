Exterior Finishes

Roofing, siding, painting, and trim — exterior work that protects your home and holds up to Connecticut weather.

Request a Consultation

Your home's exterior is its first line of defense against Connecticut's harsh winters, wet springs, and everything in between. At Restoricon, LLC, we approach exterior finishes as protection work first and cosmetic work second — every roofing, siding, painting, and trim project is planned, scoped, and executed to last, with the same structured process and vetted tradespeople we bring to every job.

Roofing

Full roof replacements and repairs using dimensional shingles, architectural shingles, and ice & water barrier underlayment — installed to Connecticut building code.

Siding

Vinyl, fiber cement, and wood siding installations — including Hardie board — properly flashed and sealed for long-term moisture protection.

Exterior Painting

Exterior surface prep, priming, and painting for siding, trim, doors, shutters, and decks — using premium exterior-grade coatings built for New England conditions.

Trim & Finish Carpentry

Exterior trim, fascia, soffit, corner boards, and decorative millwork — matched to the existing home profile and finished to last through freeze-thaw cycles.

How We Approach Exterior Work

1. Roofing — Protection From the Top Down

A failing roof doesn't just leak — it causes rot, mold, and structural damage that compounds over time. We start every roofing job with a full inspection and photo documentation, then scope the work clearly so you know exactly what's being replaced and why. We install with proper ice & water shield along all valleys and eaves, drip edge, ridge vents, and flashing around every penetration — because the details are what separate a roof that lasts from one that fails in three years.

2. Siding — Sealed Against the Elements

Siding isn't just cosmetic. Improperly installed or deteriorating siding lets water infiltrate the wall system — leading to rot, mold, and insulation failure behind walls where you can't see it. We remove old siding, inspect and address any sheathing damage, install proper house wrap or moisture barrier, and then apply new siding with correctly overlapping joints, sealed penetrations, and properly flashed windows and doors. Whether it's vinyl, Hardie board, cedar shingles, or engineered wood, the installation method matters as much as the material.

3. Exterior Painting — Prep Is Everything

Exterior paint failure almost always comes back to insufficient surface preparation, not the paint itself. We scrape, sand, and prime every surface before a single drop of topcoat goes on. Bare wood gets sealed, damaged areas get repaired, and we use premium exterior-grade coatings rated for New England's freeze-thaw cycle and UV exposure. The result is a finish that bonds correctly and holds up season after season — not one that peels and blisters within two years.

4. Trim & Finish Carpentry — The Detail That Ties It Together

Exterior trim — fascia, soffit, corner boards, window surrounds, and decorative millwork — is where the overall exterior comes together. Rotted or missing trim also creates water infiltration points. We match existing profiles where possible, use PVC or primed wood trim where appropriate for longevity, and finish everything to match the broader exterior scope so the result looks intentional and complete — not patched.

Why Exterior Work Is Protection Work

Connecticut Weather Is Unforgiving

Freeze-thaw cycles, ice dams, wind-driven rain, and summer UV all attack your home's exterior year-round. Work that isn't installed correctly doesn't just look bad — it fails. Water infiltrates, structures rot, and repairs compound into much larger jobs than a properly done original installation would have cost.

We Scope Before We Start

Every exterior project gets a written scope of work and a line-item estimate before we touch anything. You know what's being done, what materials are being used, and what it costs — no vague proposals, no post-work surprise invoices. If we find additional damage during work, we document it and discuss it with you before proceeding.

Licensed & Insured Tradespeople Only

Exterior work often requires trades working at height on scaffolding or ladders. Every contractor on our jobs is licensed, insured, and screened before they're allowed on site. We don't hand exterior projects to the lowest bidder — we coordinate vetted tradespeople who do this work correctly and stand behind it.
